## 1. Opening hook

In 2018, a studio founded in 1982 resurrected a horror icon who had died a decade earlier, and 3,346,216 Indonesians bought tickets to see her. Soraya Intercine Films' *Suzzanna: Bernapas dalam Kubur* became one of the highest-grossing Indonesian horror films of all time [CITE: filmindonesia.or.id], and the franchise continued with *Suzzanna: Malam Jumat Kliwon* (2023; 2,189,363 admissions) and *Suzzanna: Santet Dosa di Atas Dosa* (2026; 1,054,864 admissions, still in release at data collection). Yet the same studio, working in the same genre with comparable promotional machinery, also released *Racun Sangga* (2024), which drew 525,034 admissions (provisional figure) — roughly 6.4 times fewer than the 2018 revival. What separates these outcomes *before either film opens*?

Trailers on YouTube offer a candidate answer, but only if they are read correctly. Indonesian audiences praise horror trailers with comments such as *"serem banget, merinding parah"* ("so scary, total goosebumps"). Standard sentiment models read such comments as negative. In horror, however, fear is not rejection; it is the product. **Fear is demand.**

## 2. Context: a horror-dominated market that the literature overlooks

Box office forecasting from online signals is an established research stream, from word-of-mouth volume [CITE: Liu 2006; Duan, Gu & Whinston 2008] to social media buzz [CITE: Asur & Huberman 2010] and trailer-comment sentiment [CITE: IEEE 2019 YouTube trailer sentiment index]. This literature, however, is anchored in Hollywood, Chinese, Korean and Indian markets [CITE: review]. Indonesia — where horror is the commercial backbone of national cinema [CITE: industry statistics] — is largely absent from high-impact journals, and existing Indonesian studies of film audiences rely mostly on surveys and PLS-SEM rather than behavioural digital traces [CITE: Indonesian eWOM studies].

Soraya Intercine Films provides an unusually informative anchor case. Active since 1982, it built its reputation on Suzzanna-era horror, revived that legacy intellectual property after 2018, and operates a horror-focused sub-label, Hitmaker Studios [CITE: company/press sources]. Its catalogue therefore contains franchise and stand-alone releases produced under similar organisational conditions, allowing within-studio comparison that controls for production and distribution capacity — a design rarely used in box office research.

## 3. Three problems with current approaches

**Language (misread fear).** Genre-specific lexical bias in sentiment analysis has been documented for English horror reviews, which contain more negative words regardless of overall evaluation [CITE: Yale thesis on IMDb genre generalisation]. Whether the same bias affects Indonesian transformer models, applied to slang-heavy, code-mixed, emoji-rich YouTube comments, is unknown. Moreover, affective polarity is not the construct of business interest: what matters is *watch intent* — the stated plan to see the film in a cinema [CITE: purchase-intent literature].

> *Preliminary evidence (to move to Results or keep as motivation):* across 9,840 trailer comments on 15 films, the off-the-shelf model `w11wo/indonesian-roberta-base-sentiment-classifier` labelled 51.2% of comments as negative, whereas a horror-aware lexicon that treats fear words as praise labelled only 10.0% as negative. Among comments the lexicon marked positive, 30.9% were classified negative by the transformer (overall agreement 42.4%). Two local LLM annotators agreed only moderately (κ = 0.54), and human gold labels are still pending, so this gap is a hypothesis to be validated, not yet a finding.

**Time (contaminated signals).** Many studies correlate cumulative metrics observed after release with final admissions, which conflates prediction with reaction: a film can accumulate likes *because* it is succeeding [CITE: endogeneity in box office–buzz studies]. Valid forecasting requires a strict pre-release cutoff (H-14, H-7, H-1). Public platforms rarely expose historical view or like counts, but comment timestamps and search-interest series can be reconstructed for any window.

> *Preliminary evidence:* cumulative trailer likes correlate with admissions (Spearman ρ = 0.571, exact p = 0.026, bootstrap 95% CI [0.04, 0.86]; n = 15), but the association does not survive multiple-testing correction (Benjamini–Hochberg q = 0.26) and weakens after controlling for release year and franchise status (partial ρ = 0.388). Off-the-shelf sentiment shows no meaningful association (|ρ| ≤ 0.12). Pre-release Google Trends alone yields ρ = 0.157, and a leave-one-out model on that single signal (MAPE 85.8%) does not beat a naive baseline (MAPE 70.3%). These null and fragile results are the motivation for the present study: volume and polarity are not enough.

**Community (invisible audiences).** Franchise films may draw on a cohesive base of returning fans whose presence sets a floor under admissions. Co-commenting networks and community detection [CITE: Blondel et al. 2008 Louvain] can make this base visible, yet network structure has not been linked to box office in Indonesian cinema, nor compared between theatrical and streaming success.

## 4. Research gap

To date, no study has jointly examined (i) genre-specific lexical bias in non-English sentiment analysis, (ii) the distinction between affective polarity and behavioural watch intent, (iii) strict pre-release temporal validity, and (iv) audience network structure around legacy horror franchises, within a horror-dominated national film market. Using Soraya Intercine Films as an anchor case, compared with its horror sub-label Hitmaker Studios and benchmarked against competing studios (MD Pictures), this study addresses these gaps under an explicit personal-data-protection framework (Indonesia's Law No. 27/2022) [CITE: UU PDP]. *(Verify every "no study" claim with a PRISMA-style search in Scopus/WoS before submission.)*

## 5. Research questions

- **RQ1 (Language):** To what extent do Indonesian sentiment models misclassify fear expressions as negative in horror trailer comments, and is a Watch Intent Index a more valid proxy for demand?
- **RQ2 (Time):** Which strictly pre-release signals — comment volume, watch intent, search interest — are associated with admissions, and at which window (H-14, H-7, H-1)?
- **RQ3 (Franchise & community):** Do Suzzanna and other franchise films show more cohesive commenter networks and a more stable audience floor than stand-alone releases, including within the same studio?
- **RQ4 (Context):** Does a culturally aware model outperform naive baselines and standard sentiment models, within Soraya's catalogue and across studios?

## 6. Contributions

1. **Theoretical** — the *fear-as-demand* concept: negative affect as a positive demand signal in genre consumption.
2. **Methodological** — a temporally clean pre-release design, including timestamp-based reconstruction of comment signals.
3. **Empirical** — a multi-signal study of Indonesia's horror market anchored on a four-decade studio.
4. **Open & ethical science** — a pseudonymised, checksummed dataset and a UU PDP–aligned research protocol.

We operationalise this agenda in SANTET (Sentiment Analysis for Nusantara Theatrical Expectation Tracking), an open research pipeline for reading Indonesian audiences in their own idiom. SANTET is an independent academic project and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any studio analysed.
